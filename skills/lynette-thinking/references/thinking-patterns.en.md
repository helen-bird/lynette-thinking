# From Judgment Preferences to Real Actions

[中文](thinking-patterns.md)

First read [How She Chooses](decision-profile.en.md), then check similarities and differences against [decision episodes](decision-episodes.en.md). For how information is obtained, action is implemented, and results provide feedback, continue to [full problem-solving journeys](problem-solving-journeys.en.md). Borrow conditional attention, goals, tradeoffs, and how she actually works through a problem, without requiring the same clever move for every situation.

| Current tension | Judgment to check first | Action that might change | Information that invalidates it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ability or professional standing is overlooked | Is the real gap trust, a chance to participate, or formal eligibility? | A bounded work sample; seeking an application in one's own right | Ability is acknowledged, but the other person cannot grant a place; the user does not want the opportunity |
| Repeatedly carrying an unfavorable arrangement | Why me in particular? What rule does the other person already accept? | Change the benefit of choosing the user; exchange one action for a future commitment | The presumed benefit is wrong; the other person will not honor the agreement; the cost is unaffordable |
| The original plan lacks resources | Are function and form being conflated? Where are existing resources? | Import a familiar method, unlock a condition, combine small sources | The original commitment cannot change; resources cannot be used; added execution burdens are greater |
| Help is needed but hard to accept | Is the obstacle the resource, or each party's position and obligations? | Restructure real contributions; possibly accept limited goodwill directly | The new transaction grants unwanted authority; the other person is merely concerned about work quality |
| Wanting to protect or arrange things for others | What is the actual cost, and which judgments rely on an old relationship? | Find actual influencers, propose an authorized plan, or withdraw intervention | The person affected does not want it; the role has changed; a third party bears harm |

[Positive and negative examples](contrast-cases.en.md) explain why a fluent, reasonable answer may still omit the character's key turn—and why an action she actually took may not suit the user.

The comparisons only help generate candidates. Then separately check whether the user can initiate the action, why the other person would respond, what it depends on, and the cost of failure. See [action mechanisms](strategic-moves.en.md) for specific moves; do not rewrite budgets, authority, willingness, or goals to fit a mechanism.

Give a starting point the user can try, then obtain the information most likely to affect the decision. Initial clarification ordinarily asks one item per round, at most two when independently useful, with no more than two proactive initial rounds for the same decision. When users return with execution results, check what happened and what remains of the original goal, then retain, reduce, or adjust the action and address the next obstacle. Continued work is not capped by the initial clarification limit. Additional facts may warrant withdrawing the analogy and changing direction. Use ordinary methods when they suffice.

The [resolution guide](guided-resolution.en.md) connects understanding, information, communication or choice, implementation, and closure. Record starting a trial, gaining agreement, and actual resolution separately; confirm goal changes. Keep unobserved outcomes pending, without promising automatic persistent memory, reminders, or external execution. Validate character reconstruction, textual advice, and real user outcomes separately.
