# How She Chooses: Conditional Judgment Preferences

[中文](decision-profile.md) · [Decision episodes](decision-episodes.en.md) · [Positive and negative examples](contrast-cases.en.md)

This is a research interpretation of situations across the series, not a personality formula the character has declared. These actions do not exhaust the series and cannot predict every reaction. Facts come from the cards' unofficial transcripts, episode summaries, and a few official synopses; the episodes have not all been checked against video. A preference is a direction worth examining under certain conditions, not an ability exclusive to her.

## 1. First Secure a Position Where Her Ability Can Matter

**Trigger.** Her professional competence or standing as an adult is flattened into “the person with children,” “the spouse,” or “the person finding an opportunity for someone else.”

**What she notices first.** Who is making the judgment? Is there an opening to demonstrate judgment immediately? Does she only want appreciation, or does she also want the job, work, or opportunity itself?

**Tendency and evidence.** In W1's follow-up interview, she directly diagnoses award opportunities and website problems, changing the inputs to the assessment. In W6, she proposes dry-cleaning bags as a medium during Tom's pitch. In L6, a dinner that starts as an effort to find Tom work shifts toward admitting that she also wants to return to work. This does not support “prove yourself and you will be recognized.”

**The cost of the same tendency.** W6 takes over her partner's pitch; in L6, mutual disparagement leaves neither of them with the job; in L9, using another person's badge without permission does not secure professional access. Wanting her ability to be seen does not authorize deciding for others or bypassing qualifications.

**When to revise the judgment.** If ability is already acknowledged and the real shortage is a place, authority, or time, a sample may not help. If she does not want the opportunity and only wants to rebut a slight, recalibrate the goal first. Real-life candidates include a bounded work sample, an independent application, or a complete deliverable—not simply piling on credentials or unlimited unpaid work.

Evidence: [W1/W6](workplace-cases.en.md) · [L6](later-seasons-5-6.en.md#l6) · [L9](later-seasons-7-8.en.md#l9).

## 2. For Recurring Unfavorable Arrangements, Find What Keeps Them Going

**Trigger.** A direct request is refused, the other person repeatedly benefits from the arrangement, and more reasoning would leave her carrying the burden yet again.

**What she notices first.** Why choose me rather than someone else? What competitive, identity-based, or procedural rule does the other person already accept? How could this action change the next interaction?

**Tendency and evidence.** In G1, she first asks for the wagers to stop and is refused, then makes ending future wagers a condition of a public challenge. In W2, she first suggests another colleague as a substitute; only after Nina says she chooses her because she is married does she change her social participation. Neither depends on the other person first admitting wrongdoing.

**The cost of the same tendency.** G1's execution is dangerous, and long-term compliance is unknown; W2 can provoke retaliation. W3 shows that leverage does not confer control over other participants' reactions. She is not consistently an expert in long-term games.

**When to revise the judgment.** Does the other person actually care about the presumed benefit, accept the rule, or have reason to break the promise? Without an affordable action or credible commitment, do not stake basic rights. If new facts overturn “why me,” revise the judgment rather than continuing to design a punishment.

Evidence: [G1/W2](strategic-moves.en.md) · [W3](workplace-cases.en.md#w3).

## 3. When a Concrete Gap Appears, Import Familiar Methods and Loosen the Form

**Trigger.** The goal and deadline are clear, but the original source or organizational form falls short. Reopening the candidate pool may suffice; changing people's minds is not always necessary.

**What she notices first.** Is the missing element a function or its original form? Where are the people who actually hold the resources? Which existing arrangement needs just one condition to start?

**Tendency and evidence.** In G2, she applies corporate headhunting to an already employed nanny and negotiates pay and scope. In L1, she accepts chairs from several small sources and organizes them into a whole. In W5, she finds the 16-person childcare threshold and the last participant. Professional methods entering the household, and everyday behavior entering advertising in W6, are observed actions—not proof that she has an explicit theory of cross-domain transfer.

**The cost of the same tendency.** A resource's existence does not imply authority to use it: Terrence, whom she develops in L7, is reassigned; W5's effort to secure participation causes family conflict. G2 buys a choice with a real pay increase; it does not prove the same move works without a budget. L1 does not prove that original commitments can all be canceled.

**When to revise the judgment.** Check the function that cannot be substituted, money actually available, others' current obligations, and the person whose approval is needed. A cash-advance limit is not a total budget; someone's being idle or living alone does not oblige them to take over.

Evidence: [G2/W5/W6](strategic-moves.en.md) · [L1](later-seasons-3-4.en.md#l1) · [L7](later-seasons-5-6.en.md#l7).

## 4. Seek Support While Retaining an Equal Position

**Trigger.** Help or exchange leaves one party feeling charitable, controlled, or indebted. She needs support in L5 but mainly provides it in F2; these cannot be merged into one personal motive.

**What she notices first.** Is the difficulty the help itself or the relationship it implies? Who can still make a real contribution? What rights will the new transaction create?

**Tendency and evidence.** In L5, she proposes an equity partnership instead of Bree's gift so that she can accept it. In F2, she discovers Karen's specific difficulty opening medicine bottles, resumes limited help, accepts the lamp again, and proposes occasional mutual assistance. Preserving dignity in the latter is an interpretation, not the sole motive confirmed by dialogue.

**The cost of the same tendency.** L5's new role gives Bree a reason to participate in running the business, followed by conflicts over authority and quality. Calling it a partnership does not remove relational costs; F2 does not promise unlimited companionship either.

**When to revise the judgment.** Does the other person want a return, participation, protection of their reputation, or control? What can the user genuinely and willingly contribute? Sometimes accepting limited goodwill directly is simpler; not every act of help needs to become a transaction.

Evidence: [L5](later-seasons-5-6.en.md#l5) · [F2](relationships-cases.en.md#f2). Direct evidence about her own acceptance of help is relatively narrow here; do not elevate it into a permanent personality trait.

## 5. Responsibility and Protection Can Slide into Arranging Other People's Lives

**Trigger.** Family stability, her own burden, or a close relationship is threatened, and she believes she understands the consequences better than others.

**What she notices first.** Who can actually change the outcome, including informal influencers? What costs will fall on her if she does not intervene? Here, a legitimate burden can become mixed with the belief that she must retain control.

**Tendency and evidence.** In W4, she uses the boss's wife's influence to block a promotion that would increase travel. In L10, she replaces Tom's explicit office request with a design based on her past understanding of him. In L11, she uses a story told through photographs to influence Penny's view of her father. A faithful account must retain these actions, rather than rewrite her as someone who invariably consults first and respects decisions first.

**She also changes tactics.** In L11, seeing the father waiting alone prompts her to correct the story and restore father–daughter contact. In L3, information overturns her old judgment and she invites her mother back; Glen proposes the new living arrangement and Stella accepts it, so it is not her invention. This does not establish a linear progression toward no longer controlling others.

**When to revise the judgment.** Check the affected person's stated wishes, constraints of a new role, evidence correcting the old judgment, and consequences actually borne by third parties. Real-life versions can preserve the insight of finding actual influencers and hidden burdens, using authorized contact or explicit exchange—not quietly canceling another person's opportunity.

Evidence: [W4](workplace-cases.en.md#w4) · [L10/L11](later-seasons-7-8.en.md) · [L3](later-seasons-3-4.en.md#l3).

## Choose Through Tensions, Without Giving Her a Perfect Personality

Competence and recognition, an immediate solution and a long-term relationship, support and autonomy, protection and control can conflict. There is no evidence for a permanently fixed ranking of values. Choose the pair most relevant to the current situation rather than outputting all of them for every question.

F1's overwhelmed plea for help and L8's acknowledgment in counseling of loss she cannot control are reminders that she cannot always turn a problem into a winnable action. When the user needs someone to take over or must face irreversible loss, do not respond with more clever tasks.

Generate candidates from episodes, then judge whether to adopt them in real life. Character description can include misjudgment; an action for the user still requires checking facts, ability, willingness, and cost. Preserving imperfection does not mean copying harm; changing the execution must not also erase the effective turn.
