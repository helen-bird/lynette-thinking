# Decision episodes: when she changes tactics and what she accepts

[中文](decision-episodes.md) · [Decision preferences](decision-profile.en.md) · [Concrete moves](strategic-moves.en.md)

These episodes retain triggers, actions, tradeoffs, and counterevidence. **Facts** are supported by public unofficial transcripts, scene descriptions, and episode summaries. **Stated goals** are what a character says at that moment, not necessarily her entire motivation. **Interpretations** are researchers’ accounts of mechanisms. Episodes were not all checked against video, and later outcomes do not establish prior foresight. Use the relevant episode to understand a choice; do not present every card to the user. The information at the end of each card guides follow-up selection, not an additional questionnaire. Ask only about unknowns the current route actually depends on; users should not need to provide a full relationship history before receiving a starting point.

## D1 | S2E14: one challenge in exchange for future rules

- **Trigger:** Tom bonds with Ed over fraternity experience, then becomes caught in humiliating wagers and nicknames. Lynette asks for them to stop; Ed refuses on the grounds that he runs the company. A direct request has actually failed.
- **Action / change:** She issues a challenge, publicly agreeing that completing it will bring an office moratorium on wagers. She eats a pound of raw bacon; Ed agrees to stop, and she then asks for a bucket.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Stopping the wagers is explicit. Putting future constraints into a wager using Ed’s accepted competitive format is a mechanism interpretation. Witnesses are present, but there is no evidence she foresaw every reaction. She does not first obtain agreement with her values; she obtains a commitment through a mutually recognized format.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She accepts a bodily cost for change. The danger is real, yet is not essential to a real-life adaptation. Only the immediate promise is established, not permanent compliance.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Does the other person recognize the agreement, can it be enforced, and is the action affordable? If the promise is unreliable, basic protection must not depend on winning one challenge.

## D2 | S1E09: bring professional recruiting into the household

- **Trigger:** Finding a nanny has been unsuccessful. Bree can tell her where affluent families’ nannies spend time; a suitable person need not currently be looking for work.
- **Action / change:** She approaches the employed Claire with a 20% raise and overtime pay. Claire negotiates scope, including no errands for friends, telephone duties, or laundry; they reach an agreement.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Finding a nanny and the headhunting analogy have textual support. Expanding a supply pool previously excluded by default, then trading pay and scope for a voluntary choice, interprets the action.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She pays more and accepts work boundaries; words alone do not produce the resource. Immediate hiring proves neither lasting stability nor that everyone can afford a higher offer. Claire’s counterterms matter too: she is not a resource to be reassigned, and the arrangement must change her circumstances as well.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** The actual budget, candidate’s concerns, existing obligations, and channels of access. If money is unavailable, identify something genuinely exchangeable rather than assume willingness to leave a job.

## D3 | S2E05: establish why she, specifically, is being chosen

- **Trigger:** Nina repeatedly demands her company at bars and links refusal to project assignments. Lynette suggests Trudy instead. Nina explicitly prefers the married Lynette because she will not compete for attention.
- **Action / change:** She changes how she participates, dancing and attracting attention so that she no longer supplies the background role Nina wanted. Narration presents her as escaping the task.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Her wish to stop accompanying Nina and Nina’s selection criterion are directly supported. Changing the special benefit that sustains the demand is an interpretation; no additional hidden psychology is necessary. The shift follows an explicit explanation; do not skip verification and imagine every boss is competing for attention.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She competes instead of waiting for her boss to understand, potentially spending relationship capital. Immediate success establishes neither absence of retaliation nor permission to do poor work deliberately.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Is she selected for private convenience, scarce expertise, or contractual responsibility? Different reasons require different responses. Changing a social benefit may do nothing; verify rather than treat a guess as knowledge.

## D4 | S2E01: make competence visible within a short window

- **Trigger:** Nina doubts a mother’s commitment to work. Tom’s back injury means Lynette brings Penny to the interview, while Ed is about to leave for a flight.
- **Action / change:** While handling her child, she identifies award opportunities and navigation problems on the company’s website. Ed hires her immediately.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Seeking the job is explicit. Using a small piece of actual work to change the basis of evaluation is an interpretation. There is no evidence that she considered and rejected every conventional alternative. Her observations address the business rather than demonstrate greater hardship; the child’s presence is a constraint, not a prop for winning.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** Simultaneous care and demonstration are stressful; hiring does not establish sustainable overload. Ed’s acceptance does not mean she knew all his selection criteria in advance.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Is the decision-maker present? Is the missing piece evidence of ability, or a vacancy or authorization? Will the sample become excessive unpaid labor? More proof may not solve a constraint once competence is acknowledged.

## D5 | S3E15: after a mistake, preserve what must function tonight

- **Trigger:** A mistaken order number brings 50 high chairs only hours before opening. Replacing the complete set in time is impossible.
- **Action / change:** She accepts a source offering only five chairs, continues adding small supplies, and sends employees searching. The restaurant opens with mismatched seats; she apologizes to Tom. She does not dismiss the five-chair offer as a failure: a source can reduce the gap without filling it alone.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Having seats that evening is the action’s goal. Separating necessary function from a perfect appearance is an interpretation, not proof of prior optimization.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She accepts mismatched furniture and coordination costs. She had already asked Andrew to leave caring for his mother temporarily to help before discovering the chair error; that recruitment must not be presented as the chair-repair tactic. Mobilizing people has costs. Tom does not instantly cease being disappointed.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Which functions and promises cannot change, who handles transport and returns, and what gap remains? Consistency cannot be downgraded unilaterally if it is itself a core promise. Opening tonight does not prove long-term profitability.

## D6 | S1E19: an observable obstacle changes an earlier refusal

- **Trigger:** Karen extends a gift into requests for company; Lynette returns the lamp. Later, an unusually uncollected package leads her to check on Karen.
- **Action / change:** She discovers arthritis makes pill bottles difficult to open, opens them, and proposes a pharmacy visit the next day. Karen resents obligation-based help; Lynette still proposes occasional mutual help and takes the lamp again.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Helping each other without becoming friends is explicit. Preserving dignity is not an established sole motive. The change follows a concrete clue; she did not understand everything from the beginning. She does not first promise to meet all of Karen’s loneliness; specific help coexists with an honest lack of closeness.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She accepts one obligation, not unlimited closeness. The ending confirms she arrives, not that new bottles were obtained. Entering without permission is not a transferable default.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** What exact step is impossible, can a tool solve it, what support already exists, and what capacity does she have? A nonexistent obstacle or one beyond her ability does not justify indefinite responsibility.

## D7 | S2E11: apologize, retain the request, then change the test

- **Trigger:** Gaby kisses Tom. Lynette says it makes her uncomfortable, then makes excessive accusations. She visits to apologize for overreacting; Gaby still insists the original kiss was harmless.
- **Action / change:** She kisses Carlos, putting Gaby in the corresponding relationship position. Gaby understands, but Carlos mistakes it for interest in an affair, which Lynette rejects.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Wanting Gaby to stop kissing Tom that way and admitting her overreaction are explicit. Testing a one-way standard through a symmetrical experience interprets the action; candid communication alone does not describe it. The apology concerns the extra accusations; the original discomfort does not disappear with it. Record them separately.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She accepts an uncomfortable scene, but also involves a third person’s body and relationship in the demonstration, creating a new misunderstanding. It is not a costless mutual win.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Is the disagreement about a reciprocal standard or genuinely different preferences? A real-life comparison needs consent, low cost, and an exit. Demonstration cannot force shared preferences.

## D8 | S2E09: leverage does not control a third person’s next move

- **Trigger:** Coworkers promise to oppose Nina but do not follow through. Lynette then discovers Nina’s private involvement with Stu.
- **Action / change:** She rejects a raise and larger office, asking for better treatment of colleagues. Nina instead fires Stu. Lynette encourages him to seek reinstatement; he sues independently. The payout is followed by layoffs, while Lynette is promoted.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Better treatment and correcting the dismissal have support, and she says this was not her intended result. A planned promotion contradicts the evidence. Recognizing leverage is not predicting the response correctly.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** Using private information exposes colleagues and the company to risk. Allies, opponents, and beneficiaries have their own choices; personal benefit does not equal success on the original goal. Keep agency separate: she prompts Stu to act, but does not choose his lawsuit or the company’s layoffs.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Who will actually act, what do third parties want, and how can an opponent retaliate? If a plan requires everyone to follow her script, they are not resources already under her control.

## D9 | S5E14–16: replace a gift with investment, and new power enters too

- **Trigger:** Bree offers $20,000 when money is short. Lynette explicitly fears that inability to repay, or an unequal gift exchange, will harm their friendship.
- **Action / change:** She proposes a 15% restaurant interest, with profit participation and repayment on liquidation. Bree later invokes partnership to intervene in food and staffing, escalating conflict. The returned check, mutual admissions, and reconciliation come in E16, after the restaurant is in liquidation.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Avoiding damage to friendship is explicit. Retaining a position in which she contributes and stands more equally is an interpretation. She accepts differently structured help rather than refusing all help. Initial agreement does not establish a shared understanding of operating authority; the later conflict reveals the difference.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She exchanges personal indebtedness for real ownership rights, not freedom from obligation. The collision, insults, and later repair remain part of the story; it is not simply successful collaboration.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** What participation does the other person expect, who decides quality and spending, and do the new rights cost more than the gift? Renaming an arrangement does not resolve power conflicts.

## D10 | S6E13–14: seeking help becomes more than a defense

- **Trigger:** Tom suggests counseling; she refuses. After he goes alone, she fears being described as a bad wife and attends to defend herself.
- **Action / change:** When the discussion reaches uncontrollable life events and losing a child, she sits and continues, later agreeing to joint counseling. In the following episode she says difficult decisions stall unless she takes them on. Tom admits wanting to be liked and leaving hard tasks to her.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Defending herself and carrying difficult decisions are explicit statements. Her initial fear about Tom’s account is not established fact. Some burden is corroborated; that does not excuse every harsh action. Do not erase the initial defensiveness and turn later acceptance of support into an ability she always possessed.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She allows loss to be heard, yet also displaces another client’s appointment. Later, mockery gets them expelled. Understanding and apology do not prove lasting repair of the division of responsibility.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** Who proposes, announces, and deals with difficult decisions, and can support genuinely take over? More clever tactics may burden someone facing uncontrollable loss. Poor stage acting is not evidence of poor counseling expertise.

## D11 | S7E20: old familiarity overrides an explicit present choice

- **Trigger:** Tom commissions an office and explicitly requests an imposing style. Lynette thinks this is not the real Tom.
- **Action / change:** She secretly substitutes a comfortable design. Renee restores the requested version; Tom likes it, but Lynette still objects. He describes the authority required by his new role; she says she does not want to lose the man she married.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Her claim to know what he really wants and wish to retain the familiar relationship are textual. Jealousy of his success is someone else’s interpretation, not established psychology.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She overrides the client and collaborator, damaging cooperation and trust. Renee supplies the accepted version. This does not support infallible insight into underlying needs, and no immediate repair is shown. Client satisfaction and her disapproval coexist; her disapproval cannot erase the feedback or make her alternative a success.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** New role requirements, stated wishes, and feedback contradicting old assumptions. Familiarity can generate a question, but does not replace present decision rights.

## D12 | S8E07: seeing whom a strategy hurts, she gives up a short-term win

- **Trigger:** Jane’s closeness to Penny unsettles her. Initially unwilling to involve the child, she later uses old photographs and a narrative that casts Jane as taking the family away.
- **Action / change:** Penny withdraws from her father. Seeing Tom waiting alone, Lynette acknowledges her influence, takes Penny to the park, corrects the claim that her father abandoned the family for Jane, and apologizes for what she should not have said.
- **Goal and interpretation:** Both the initial principle and later correction have explicit support. The first half contradicts a claim that she always protects the child’s choices first. The change follows concrete estrangement, not simply altered odds of winning. She corrects an account she created rather than accepting all responsibility for the separation; repair does not guarantee his return.
- **Tradeoff / counterevidence:** She gives up an alliance favorable to herself; father and daughter reconnect while she remains hurt. Neither marital reunion in that episode nor emotional resolution follows.
- **Information that changes the judgment:** What actual cost is a third party bearing, and has her account distorted their choice? Hypothetical harm alone cannot require a user to abandon a legitimate interest; temporary effectiveness does not make a tactic worth continuing.
