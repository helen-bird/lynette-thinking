# How the Skill is built

[简体中文](development-method.md) · [Character sources](evidence.en.md) · [Home](../README.md)

The starting point is a concrete choice Lynette makes in *Desperate Housewives*: what she notices, what she wants to preserve, whom she asks, what she changes, and what happens afterward. The Skill follows that process through to action and consequences.

## Follow the whole problem

The [problem-solving journeys](../skills/lynette-thinking/references/problem-solving-journeys.en.md) trace information gathering, negotiation, resources, implementation, feedback, and correction. The [decision episodes](../skills/lynette-thinking/references/decision-episodes.en.md) separate plot facts, explicitly stated goals, and our interpretation of the mechanism. An action's eventual outcome does not mean she foresaw every consequence.

## Preserve the person behind the move

The [decision profile](../skills/lynette-thinking/references/decision-profile.en.md) keeps her professional ambition, competitiveness, desire for an equal position, and tendency to take over. Her mistakes matter too: knowing someone well can turn into deciding for them, and accepting help can introduce new power. These tensions help determine when a move fits.

The [contrasts](../skills/lynette-thinking/references/contrast-cases.en.md) show which details change the judgment. The [concrete moves](../skills/lynette-thinking/references/strategic-moves.en.md) then help turn that judgment into an action, such as changing a commitment, finding why someone keeps choosing you, or combining smaller sources of support.

## Translate the insight into the user's next step

The assistant starts with the user's goal, current obstacle, known facts, and constraints. It offers a manageable starting point, then asks only for information likely to change the decision. When that information is outside the conversation, it helps identify whom to ask and how.

A useful proposal needs an owner, available resources, a reason others might accept, and a cost the user can bear. A character's action may be adopted, adapted, or rejected in reality. An ordinary solution is welcome when it works; novelty is optional.

When the user returns with what happened, the [resolution guide](../skills/lynette-thinking/references/guided-resolution.en.md) connects that feedback to the original goal and the next obstacle. A proposal, agreement, trial, and lasting result are distinct. Changing the goal requires the user's agreement.
